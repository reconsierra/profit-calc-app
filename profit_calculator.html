Customer Profit Calculator — Field Reference

Quick-reference talking points for explaining the calculator to a workshop owner or manager.

What this tool does

Shows a workshop owner the extra turnover and profit available from selling a set of commonly used service add-ons — wiper blades, additives, brake supplies and more — alongside every service.

The one-line pitch

"This is a demonstration — it shows the kind of extra turnover and profit your shop could generate from items you're likely already selling occasionally, just by tracking and applying them consistently."

Three things to always mention

  • It's indicative, not a guarantee. The figures are built from common service items and typical attach rates — a realistic illustration, not an audited forecast.
  • It excludes labour, overheads and the service job itself. This is purely the extra margin from add-on items — not total shop profit.
  • Attach rates matter. Wiper blades and brake paste aren't sold on every car — the tool lets you set a realistic % so the numbers stay credible.

If they ask about the "workshop supplies charge" line

It's not tied to one product. It covers the small incidental items used on most cars — rags, car wash consumables, screws, clips, and the many other bits bought in from suppliers like Würth.

Frame it as a fair-share cost recovery: every car serviced carries a small share of what it costs to keep that stock on hand — this line simply recovers that share.

How to run it in 30 seconds

  • Set their average cars/day and markup %.
  • Tick the items they already stock or could easily stock.
  • Adjust wiper blade / brake paste % to a realistic attach rate for their shop.
  • Point to the annual turnover and annual profit figures at the bottom — that's the conversation starter.

Remember: the goal is to open a conversation about consistent upselling, not to promise an exact dollar figure.
